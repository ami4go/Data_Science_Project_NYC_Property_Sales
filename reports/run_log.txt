===== 05_eda.py =====
exploration rows 590,043; market rows 397,390 = 382,004 deals; valid price per sq ft 174,115

f01_price_classes: 30.8% of recorded sales are $0 and 32.3% are <= $100 (deed transfers, not prices), so price statistics must exclude them. Counting each multi-lot deal once (382,004 market deals), prices have median $738K, IQR $456K-$1.2M and max $2.4B: right-skewed over five orders of magnitude, so we analyse log price and use rank-based alternatives.

f02_transfer_share_by_borough: Nominal transfers range from 21.2% of recorded sales in Manhattan to 37.5% in Brooklyn (16.4 percentage points apart, well beyond the 2-point threshold): motivates T4 (chi-square test of independence).

f03_missingness: Gross floor area is missing for 44.4% of sales, but that is 95% of condo/co-op unit sales vs 5% of other sales: it is missing by design (the area belongs to the building, not the unit), not at random. Per-sq-ft results therefore speak for houses, rental and commercial buildings, not apartments; we do not impute it.

f04_year_built_heaping: 60.8% of parcels have a year built ending in 0 or 5 (20% expected without rounding); 37.9% end in 0. Among pre-1940 parcels it is 73.2%. 1940 itself holds 12,337 parcels vs 540 in 1939, so a building recorded as 1940 may really be pre-war: T1 needs a sensitivity check with the cutoff moved (e.g. drop 1935-1945).

f05_price_trends: Manhattan has the highest median deal price every year ($999K-$1.2M), the obvious answer, but from 2016 to 2025 it rose 22% vs 48-71% in the other boroughs. Indexed to 2019, Manhattan's price per sq ft was 107 in 2021 vs 116 for the Brooklyn-Queens average and 81 vs 128 in 2025 (P2; 1-3 family and rental buildings only, so the type mix is the same every year). Caveat: Manhattan has only 270-567 such sales a year in this set (its market is condos and co-ops, which carry no area), so T2 uses log price of like-for-like apartment sales instead.

f06_prewar_vs_newer: Pooled over the city, pre-war residential buildings sell for +6.6% per sq ft (geometric mean) vs newer ones, but within the same neighbourhood and building type the median pre-war premium is +1.6% across 383 cells (57% of cells positive, IQR -6% to +11%). Holding location and type fixed moves the gap from +6.6% to +1.6%, near the 5% 'matters' threshold, so the answer could go either way: P1 is not obvious, and T1 must compare like with like.

f07_size_vs_price: Spearman rho = 0.49 between floor area and price for 153,157 1-3 family sales: bigger homes do cost more (the obvious question), but only moderately, since location varies more than size. Price per sq ft falls with size (rho = -0.26), so per-sq-ft comparisons must hold building type (and so size) fixed.

f08_sales_volume: Market deals in Apr-Jun 2020 were -48% vs Apr-Jun 2019; the busiest month was 2021-08 (4,763 deals in this 70% set). The COVID period changes which properties sell, a composition effect P2 must keep in mind; transfers track market deals month to month.

f09_top_neighbourhoods: Among neighbourhoods with >= 100 residential per-sq-ft sales, Greenwich Village-West (Manhattan) is priciest at $2,020/sq ft; 8 of the top 15 are in Manhattan and 7 in Brooklyn. Neighbourhood explains far more than borough, so tests should condition on it.

f10_toll_zone: 54% of geocoded Manhattan market deals fall inside the zone. Counting each deal once, the median price inside went $1.25M -> $1.27M (+1.6%) from 2024 to 2025, outside $1.07M -> $1.15M (+6.6%). Counting every lot of multi-lot deals would instead show +10.4% inside vs +5.1% outside; the largest, one $170M deal repeated on 280 lots in 2025, weighs as 280 sales, so price statistics use one row per deal. Quarterly medians are noisy (880-1,443 deals per cell), and the condo/co-op mix differs inside and outside, so T3 compares like types.

f11_price_by_borough: Median deal price: Manhattan $1.2M, Brooklyn $885K, Queens $620K, Bronx $514K, Staten Island $569K. The boxes overlap heavily, so borough alone is a weak summary of price.

f12_correlations: Log price rises with floor area (rho = 0.51) and units (rho = 0.26); year built vs log $/sq ft is only rho = -0.14 citywide, and latitude/longitude vs log $/sq ft are -0.03/-0.02: location matters (f09) but not in a straight line north-south, so it must enter as neighbourhood, not coordinates. Rank correlations (pairwise complete, multi-lot rows left out) because every variable is skewed; associations, not causes.

# Hypothesis test results (confirmation set, 30%, seed 42)

Plan fixed in advance: `reports/test_plan.md`. Rows: 252,178. alpha = 0.05.

## T1 (P1, primary): pre-war premium per sq ft, within neighbourhood x type
* Rows: 71,036 confirmation sales with a valid price per sq ft and year built (1-3 family and rental buildings); 367 cells with >= 5 pre-war and >= 5 newer sales (58,139 sales).
* **Estimate:** mean within-cell premium +0.43% (95% CI -1.68% to +2.58%); mean d = 0.0043 log points, s_d = 0.2068.
* **Paired t-test (one-sided):** t = 0.394, d.f. = 366, p = 0.347 -> do not reject H0 at alpha = 0.05.
* **Does it matter (5%)?** does not matter: the whole interval is inside +/- the threshold.
* Robustness, without year built 1935-1945: +2.06% (95% CI -0.32% to +4.49%), t = 1.698, d.f. = 346, p = 0.0452 (347 cells).
* Alternatives on all 71,036 sales, ignoring neighbourhood: F = 1.600 (p = 0) for equal variances; pooled t = 16.28, d.f. = 71034, p = 8.24e-60, gap +7.11%; Welch t = 16.75, d.f. = 71025, p = 3.61e-63, gap +7.11%.

## T3 (P4 upgraded): $0-$100 transfers by borough, holding building type fixed
* Rows: 252,178 confirmation deeds; citywide transfer rate per building category (47 categories) gives each borough's expected transfers.
* **Chi-square goodness of fit:** chi2 = 461.3, d.f. = 4, p = 1.54e-98 -> reject H0. Smallest expected count 7,873 (>= 5).
  * Bronx: observed 32.1% vs expected 37.0% -> -4.9 pp (95% CI -5.5 to -4.3); O/E = 0.867.
  * Brooklyn: observed 37.7% vs expected 35.8% -> +2.0 pp (95% CI +1.6 to +2.3); O/E = 1.055.
  * Manhattan: observed 21.1% vs expected 21.3% -> -0.2 pp (95% CI -0.5 to +0.2); O/E = 0.992.
  * Queens: observed 35.4% vs expected 34.0% -> +1.4 pp (95% CI +1.1 to +1.7); O/E = 1.041.
  * Staten Island: observed 32.6% vs expected 38.1% -> -5.5 pp (95% CI -6.1 to -4.9); O/E = 0.855.
* Does it matter (2 pp)? 2 of 5 boroughs are 2 pp or more from their expected share.
* Alternative, raw chi-square of independence (ignores building type): chi2 = 4,472.5, d.f. = 4, p = 0; raw shares Bronx 32.1%, Brooklyn 37.7%, Manhattan 21.1%, Queens 35.4%, Staten Island 32.6% (spread 16.6 pp).
* Caveat: expected counts use rates estimated from the same data, so d.f. = 4 is approximate.
